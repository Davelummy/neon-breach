# Campaign lighting, camera and city-life foundation

Campaign missions allow both day and night; Night Raptor no longer locks night or automatically activates NVG. Day/Night is visible in the briefing and can also be changed through Pause → Lighting & camera without restarting. Explicit daily-challenge night rules remain separate from ordinary missions.

Night vision is a separate opt-in green imaging mode: B, controller Share/Create, MORE → NVG, or the pause controls. V, controller Touchpad, MORE → VIEW, or pause switches first/third person. Third person renders the existing tactical player rig and shortens the rear camera when tall geometry would obstruct it. Vehicle chase cameras and finisher cameras retain their own behavior. Keyboard/mouse, shooting, jet and controller vehicle controls are preserved.

Each fictional Abuja-inspired district has up to fifteen non-combat civilian residents around busy corridors. They walk short collision-safe routes, pause, and hurry when nearby gunfire occurs. They are not enemy targets, do not award kills or credits, and do not appear as enemy contacts. Their movement updates at ten Hz with shared simple geometry and distance culling; low limits visible civilian models to six. This is ambient city life, not a complete civilian economy, traffic simulation or photorealistic character system.

The District Armory is an in-world counter marked by a cyan square on the map. Approach on foot and use E/Triangle/the contextual Shop button. Vehicles keep interaction priority if one is close. Shopping pauses the campaign. Hostile eliminations earn twenty local credits; the career-locked marksman rifle can alternatively be bought for 240 credits. Already-unlocked equipment can be selected there without a purchase. Purchases deduct once, refuse insufficient funds and require successful storage before equipping. Credits and purchased ownership are account-scoped on this browser, not cloud-synced, server-authoritative, monetized or valid for multiplayer. No payment integration or real-money sale exists.

Checkpoint saves retain camera mode, explicit NVG state and civilians, with validation and compatibility for older saves. Multiplayer has not been silently given these campaign-only systems. The Canvas compatibility renderer remains limited; new third-person/civilian world visuals require genuine WebGL2.

Norman principles used: keep lighting distinct from imaging mode; expose conditions before deployment; show contextual shop prices, balance and purchase outcome; preserve familiar interaction mapping; avoid taking the vehicle action away when introducing the shop. These choices are functionally tested, not validated comprehension studies.

Local checks: unit tests cover day/night freedom, bounded wallet/purchases, each district's civilians and store, and separation from vehicle spawns. Campaign browser QA verifies genuine 3D, opt-in NVG, third person, changing Night Raptor to day without restart, civilians/store, purchase deduction and ownership after reload, plus existing vehicle, jet, pickup and exact-save regressions. Physical iPad/DualSense testing and fresh hardware-backed FPS validation remain needed. Screenshots are kept in ignored `.codex/artifacts/city-life*.png`.

A high-fidelity design pass still needs purpose-built Abuja architecture, civilian clothing/animations, coherent street materials and authored environmental detail. Procedural geometry is not a claim of AAA realism. No assets were downloaded, packages installed, production deployment made or merge performed for this pass.

## Shared driving pass

Campaign and multiplayer use `vehicle-handling.js` for progressive acceleration, smoothed speed-dependent steering, natural coast-down, service braking and limited reverse. Parked vehicles cannot rotate in place. Buses accelerate more slowly and turn less sharply; kekes turn more tightly; taxis retain the higher straight-line speed. Boost works forwards only and consumes the existing bounded fuel. Pulling back brakes before reverse; L2/right-click/touch Brake provides a dedicated stop action. Touch brake state clears when entering/leaving vehicles. Campaign right-stick/mouse/touch look no longer steers the vehicle; steering stays on the movement controls. The campaign chase camera smooths movement and heading, with a modest speed-dependent FOV and speed/drive/reverse/braking feedback. This remains bounded arcade driving, not a tire/suspension simulator. Collision authority, hull damage, vehicle impact eliminations and safe exits are preserved.
